# Honest scenarios and risks

Oct 29, 2026 · @emlyn

## The honest answer

The full plan was simulated 1,000,000 times per scenario: Formcraft, Soko Foods with all its arms, Logistics, Nyumba and the Trade Assistant, each run with AI, each with a YouTube channel feeding an automated sales funnel, and each expanding in stages from Kampala to Uganda, East Africa, Africa and the world. Combined revenue passes USD 1 billion by 2047 (age 45) in **7.8%** of runs, by age 55 in **27%**, and before age 70 in **40%**. With better practice (stop rules, a new venture every five years, failure risk cut 30%) it reaches **83%**.

Those numbers rest on three assumptions stacked together: AI cuts failure risk by 15%, YouTube visibility cuts it a further 8% and adds growth, and expansion beyond Uganda keeps growth going when one market fills up. Without AI or YouTube the same five ventures give 10.5%. Each assumption is tested on its own below, and each must be proven with real numbers in the first three years.

&#91;embedded content: Monte Carlo, 10,000 runs, this profile's model (method below)\]

That is not bad news. Of companies founded worldwide over 20 years, Bain counts [fewer than 250 that crossed USD 1 billion in revenue out of about 225,000 venture-backed starts](https://www.bain.com/insights/herd-on-the-street-so-many-unicorns-so-little-cash/), roughly 1 in 900. Five ventures, two of them already moving, give much better odds than one.

&#91;embedded content: Same 10,000 runs\]

**What the runs say, in plain terms.**

- **The middle case is now large.** Half the runs end with USD 546M or more of combined revenue by age 70.
- **The bottom is still real.** In 1.4% of runs everything fails early and revenue stays under USD 1M; in 6% it stays under USD 10M.
- **The top is very wide.** The best 10% of runs pass USD 14 billion. Big outcomes still come from one or two ventures compounding across many countries.

## Five simulations

Each simulation asks a different question. Together they show that the plan's odds depend less on growth than on survival and on what happens to time and money after a venture fails.

| Simulation | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Monte Carlo | How likely is the goal, with real failure rates? |
| 2. Sensitivity | Which single change moves the odds most? |
| 3. Stress test | What if a recession or currency shock hits all ventures at once? |
| 4. Stop rules | Does closing weak ventures early help? |
| 5. Venture factory | What if failed or sold ventures are replaced with new ones? |

Two deterministic checks sit beside them: the goal-seek and the combinations above.

**1. Monte Carlo.** The odds chart at the top: 11% before age 70 as planned.

**2. Sensitivity.** Losing AI cuts the odds from 40% to 17%, the largest single loss; losing Soko Foods cuts them to 24%, and losing YouTube's visibility to 29%. Halving the yearly failure risk lifts them to 71% (chart in How this was tested).

&#91;embedded content: Simulations 3–5; each row changes one thing from the plan\]

**3. Stress test.** A shock raises every venture's failure risk by 8 points and cuts that year's revenue by a quarter. In 7% of years (about once a decade), the odds fall to 29.5%; in 15% of years, to 19.8%. Revenue in more than one country and currency is the defence.

**4. Stop rules.** Closing any venture that runs at under half its plan two years running, and moving its time to the others, now lifts the odds to 46% and the middle result to USD 646M. With five ventures and AI, freed time has somewhere useful to go.

**5. Venture factory.** A new venture every five years from 2032, sized like a typical start (USD 0.05M in year one to 1.5M by year four, 60% launch), including one-off ventures built to be sold. It lifts the odds to 53%; with stop rules and failure risk also cut by 30%, to **83%**, with a middle result of USD 12 billion. Treat that as the ceiling of a perfectly run portfolio, not a forecast.

**Other simulations worth running next.**

- **Annual updating:** each January, replace the guesses with the real numbers and rerun. The odds will move fast in the first three years.
- **Ugandan data:** swap the US failure rates for URA or UBOS business-register data, if obtainable.
- **Cash-flow simulation:** model each venture's cash month by month, since most deaths are cash deaths.
- **Exit valuation:** price what a stake in Logistics or Nyumba might sell for, separate from revenue.

## What each assumption is worth

The jump from 10.5% to 40% comes from three assumptions, and AI is worth the most of them. Each one should be tested against real numbers by 2029: costs per job with and without AI, leads per month from YouTube, and revenue from outside Uganda.

&#91;embedded content: AI, YouTube and expansion settings are assumptions to be proven, listed in How this was tested\]

- **No AI, no YouTube:** 10.5%; middle lifetime revenue USD 66M.
- **YouTube only:** 16.7%. Visibility brings customers, but every venture still needs a full team.
- **AI only:** 29.2%. AI lets Emlyn and small teams do the work of large ones, the way Formcraft's tools were built.
- **AI and YouTube, Uganda only:** 32.5%.
- **AI, YouTube and global expansion (the full plan):** 40%; middle result USD 546M.
- **Full plan, but Emlyn runs everything himself:** 28%. Overstretch costs more than going global adds.

**Time to go deep.** Every venture needs Emlyn's attention to detail once, then a leader and AI tooling so it runs without him. A workable rhythm:

- **One deep venture a quarter,** in this order for 2027–2028: Formcraft, Logistics' first bike, Soko's pilot, Nyumba's CEO search.
- **Each venture gets a leader,** its AI tooling, its YouTube channel and its website funnel before he moves on.
- **A weekly hour per venture** for numbers and decisions; everything else runs through its leader.

## How to do it better

The simulations reward the same few habits, in this order.

1. **Stay alive first.** Six months of costs in reserve per venture, low fixed costs, no single client above 30% of revenue. This is the biggest lever in every run.
2. **Hit the 2031 gate.** USD 11.2M combined that year; check it yearly against the needed-numbers table.
3. **Get to three working ventures.** Soko Foods plus two others; Soko is in every fast combination.
4. **Recycle, don't just stop.** When a venture is closed or sold, start the next within a year, using what was learned.
5. **Build to sell where it fits.** Logistics and Nyumba can be built for an exit; the cash funds the next venture.
6. **Spread across countries.** Kenya and Rwanda revenue softens a Ugandan shock.
7. **One CEO per venture.** Emlyn runs Formcraft; the others need their own leaders by 2029.
8. **Rerun the model every January.** Replace guesses with actuals, and act on the result.

## What to track

The model gets better only when guesses are replaced by real numbers. Each venture tracks its own set monthly; the January review uses them to rerun the simulation. Each line gives the metric, then the healthy level and the warning level.

**Every venture.**

- **Cash runway:** months of costs in the bank. Healthy 6+; warning under 3.
- **Revenue growth:** year on year. Healthy at or above the needed-numbers path; warning under half of it.
- **Gross margin:** revenue minus direct costs, as a share of revenue.
- **Net margin:** profit after all costs.
- **Receivables days:** how long clients take to pay. Healthy under 45; warning over 90.
- **Largest client's share of revenue.** Healthy under 30%; warning over 50%.
- **Revenue per employee.**
- **Customer acquisition cost and lifetime value.** Healthy when lifetime value is 3 times the cost or more.
- **Repeat and referral rate.**

**Formcraft (design, build, workshop, software).**

- **Proposal win rate.** Healthy 30%+.
- **Backlog:** signed work not yet done, in months of revenue. Healthy 3+.
- **Average design fee per project** and **fee per square metre.**
- **Staff utilisation:** billable hours over paid hours. Healthy 65–80%.
- **Project margin against estimate.** Warning when jobs finish more than 10% over cost.
- **Change orders per project** and **defects and rework.**
- **Safety incidents.** Target zero.
- **Tools: monthly recurring revenue, paying outside firms, churn.**
- **Public tenders: bids submitted, shortlisted, won; days to get paid.**

**Soko Foods (grains, flours, farms).**

- **Tonnes bought and sold per month.**
- **Spread per kilogram:** selling price minus buying price, by crop.
- **Spoilage and rejection:** share of stock lost to moisture, pests or aflatoxin. Warning over 3%.
- **Stock turns:** how often the store empties and refills a year.
- **Members in group buying** and **money saved per household** against market prices. Target 5%+.
- **Farmers supplying** and **share paid on delivery.**
- **Coffee: trees planted, survival rate, kilograms per tree, price per kilogram (FAQ and Kiboko).**
- **Working capital tied up in stock.**

**Logistics.**

- **Courier: deliveries per bike per day, revenue per bike per day** (target UGX 60,000), **payback months per bike** (target under 12).
- **Courier: on-time delivery** (healthy 95%+) and **damaged or lost parcels.**

- **Repeat business clients** and **share of revenue under contract** (healthy 60%+).

- **Truck app: days from delivery to paying the trucker** (target 2 or fewer) and **cash paid out before the client paid** (target zero).
- **Truck app: empty running** (warning over 30%) and **cancellations.**

**Nyumba (renting).**

- **Rent collected on time.**

**Trade Assistant.**

## How this was tested

The same rules as the Trade Assistant: start from base rates, compare against chance, write the settings down before looking at results, and report the bad runs as loudly as the good ones.

**Settings, fixed before the run (seed 20261029).**

1. **Starting plan:** each venture follows its ramp, then its planned growth rates. Soko Foods includes its four arms (institutions, shops and online, silos, plantations); Nyumba includes the USD 5 landlord fee; the Trade Assistant sells its tools from 2028.
2. **Will it launch?** Formcraft 100%. E-boda courier 85%. Soko core 70%, each arm 40–50%. Nyumba 60%. Truck app 30%, only if the courier is alive in 2029. Trade Assistant tools 40%. YouTube channels 80%.
3. **Yearly chance of closing:** 16% in years 1–2, 10% in years 3–5, 7% to year 10, 5% after (US base rates).
4. **AI in every venture:** failure risk 15% lower, growth 2 points faster (without AI: 10% higher and 2 points slower, with software delayed).
5. **YouTube and the sales funnel:** each company runs a channel that sends viewers to an AI-automated website. Modelled as failure risk 8% lower and growth 2 points faster, from the evidence that content marketing produces leads at [about 62% lower cost](https://webfx.com/blog/marketing/inbound-marketing-statistics), plus a YouTube income stream: USD 2k in 2027 rising to USD 100k by 2031 across all channels, then 35% a year for eight years.
6. **Staged expansion:** Kampala, then Uganda, East Africa, Africa and the world. When a venture fills one market it keeps growing 9% a year (6% if it stays in Uganda), the chance of stalling falls from 8% to 6% a year, and late growth is 10% (8% if Uganda only).
7. **Execution luck and noise:** random starting size, a skill draw of ±8 points, ±15 points of yearly noise.
8. **What is counted:** revenue of the companies and channels, not profit and not Emlyn's share.

**Known weaknesses.** The failure rates are American; Ugandan small-firm data is thinner and probably worse. The ventures are treated as independent, but one recession, election or currency shock would hit all four at once, so the bad tail is understated. No inflation or exchange-rate effects are modelled.

**How many runs is enough.** Every scenario ran 1,000,000 times. The 50,000,000-run check below was made on the version before the e-boda plan; the engine is the same. Past a million, more runs only shrink the margin from chance; they cannot fix a wrong assumption. A billion runs would take about two hours here and move the answer by less than 0.01 points.

&#91;embedded content: Main plan only; the scenarios use 1,000,000 runs each\]

**Which levers move the odds.** One setting was changed at a time.

&#91;embedded content: Sensitivity runs; base differs slightly from the 8.9% above because each test uses its own random seed\]

- **AI is the biggest lever.** Without it the odds fall from 40% to 17%.
- **YouTube's visibility matters far more than its money.** Dropping the channels' income alone moves the odds by 1 point; dropping YouTube altogether, including its effect on sales, moves them by 11.
- **Soko Foods carries the most weight among the ventures.** Without it the odds fall to 24%; without Formcraft, to 30%.
- **Going beyond Uganda adds 8 points** (32.5% if the ventures stay in Uganda).
- **Overstretch undoes a third of the gain:** 28% if Emlyn tries to run everything himself.

## Formcraft: the steady base

Formcraft is the most likely to last, because it already has clients and revenue. In the middle surviving run it is a USD 2.2M-a-year firm in 2036: a strong regional business, not a giant. It is still open in 46% of runs that year.

- **Best (top 10%): USD 5.3M or more in 2036.** Tools sold to outside firms; Families and concrete scale; a Kenya office.
- **Base (middle): USD 2.2M.** Studio and Stores carry it; Tools stay mostly in-house.
- **Worst: closed.** One unpaid job or failed build drains cash; the founders split.

**What could go wrong.**

- **A large client does not pay** (medium odds, severe). 40% deposit, stage payments, no work ahead of cash.
- **Defect or site accident** (low, severe). Engineer sign-off, insurance, safety log.
- **Co-founder dispute** (medium, severe). Shareholder agreement with vesting and buy-out terms in 2027.
- **Emlyn is the only one who knows the systems** (high, severe). Written docs, a junior developer, a second admin on every system.
- **Tools never sell outside** (high, moderate). 3 paying outside firms by end-2028, or keep them in-house.
- **Price war on drawings** (high, moderate). Sell BIM and automation, not drawings by the sheet.
- **Shilling falls or materials jump** (medium, moderate). Escalation clause; quotes valid 14 days.
- **Government pays late** (high, moderate). Public work capped at 30% of revenue.
- **AI makes drawings cheap for everyone** (high, mixed). Be the firm that uses it best; sell finished outcomes.

**Stop and fix rules.**

- Cash reserve under one month for two months running: freeze hiring and new divisions.
- 2027 revenue under USD 30k (plan: 46k): cut back to Studio and Stores until it recovers.
- No outside Tools customer by end-2028: stop selling Tools; keep them for Formcraft's own use.

## Logistics: one e-boda first, a truck app second

The plan is a business delivery service in Kampala that starts with one electric boda, adds riders, then becomes an app where businesses book trucks: "SafeBoda for businesses", built for construction sites, movers and grain. Starting with one bike makes it the cheapest venture to begin and the one most likely to still exist (some part of it is running in 33% of runs in 2036). The money is in the truck app, which exists in only 7% of runs, because it is the hardest step.

**What the record says.** SafeBoda, Uganda's largest boda platform, launched a [business transport and delivery portal](https://allafrica.com/stories/202603270254.html) in March 2026 with more than 200 companies, after Kampala restricted passenger bodas in the city centre, so the courier competes with it from the first day. [Kobo360](https://techcabal.com/2025/03/04/kobo360-new-owner/), Africa's best-known "Uber for trucks", raised about USD 79M and gathered 50,000 trucks, then stalled: it paid truckers upfront while large shippers paid late, and ran on bank loans. [Only three African logistics startups raised venture money in 2024](https://techcabal.com/2025/01/20/kobo360-layoff/), USD 2.1M between them.

**Lessons built into the plan.**

- **Never pay a trucker before the client has paid.** Take payment upfront by mobile money, or hold it until delivery.
- **Start with trips already in the family:** Formcraft's materials, Soko's grain, tenants moving through Nyumba.
- **Grow with owner-drivers and contractors,** not a fleet bought on loans.
- **Win business clients on reliability and tracking,** not on price against SafeBoda.
- **Make construction the anchor market:** sand, aggregate, cement and steel move every day.

**Best, base and worst.**

- **Best (top 10%): USD 6M or more in 2036.** The truck app takes off from 2029 and is later sold to a regional logistics group.
- **Base:** a profitable courier of 20–40 bikes, about USD 0.2M a year; the app stays a booking tool.
- **Worst:** the first bike never pays back, or SafeBoda's business portal takes the clients; closed by 2029.

**What could go wrong.**

- **Bike or battery downtime** (high, moderate). Swap-network bikes; a spare battery.
- **Rider theft, accidents, damaged goods** (medium, severe). Insurance, tracking, proof-of-delivery photos.
- **City rules on bodas** (medium, severe). Delivery-only branding; follow KCCA rules closely.
- **Clients pay late** (high, severe). Prepaid bookings for small clients; deposits on contracts.
- **Truck owners will not join** (medium, severe). Guarantee fast payment after delivery; start with owners already trusted by Formcraft.
- **Funding the app** (medium, moderate). Build it in-house with Emlyn's stack; grow from courier profits.

**Stop and fix rules.**

- Bike 1 does not cover its costs within 6 months: stop before buying bike 2.
- Fewer than 10 repeat business clients by end-2028: do not build the truck app.
- Under 500 paid bookings a month after 12 months of the app, or any trip paid out before the client pays: pause and fix.

**Exit path.** If the truck app reaches about USD 5M a year, a sale at 1–1.5 times revenue is plausible; Emlyn's stake becomes cash for the next venture.

**Open questions:** who rides the first bike, the first five business clients, the brand name, and Emlyn's stake if partners join.

## Nyumba: the hardest market

Nyumba has the weakest odds of the four businesses. It needs a CEO and a pre-seed before it exists (a 60% chance in the model), and African rental platforms have a poor record. It is open in 23% of runs in 2036; if it survives, the middle run earns USD 2.3M that year.

**Tested: a USD 5 landlord connection fee.** Charging each landlord USD 5 per tenant connected adds about USD 0.1M a year by year 3 (about 20,000 connections), lifting year-3 revenue from about USD 0.73M to 0.83M, roughly 14%. In 1,000,000 runs it moves the lifetime billion odds only from 9.9% to 10.1%, but it does something more useful: it makes Nyumba's income less dependent on collection fees, and a USD 5 charge is small next to a month's rent, so landlords are unlikely to refuse it.

- **Best (top 10%): USD 7.8M or more in 2036.** Trust and the Rent Passport win landlords; expands to Nairobi and Kigali.
- **Base (middle): USD 2.4M.** Solid in Kampala; fees stay small per tenancy.
- **Worst: never launches or closes.** No CEO or no funding; or landlords and brokers refuse to switch.

**What could go wrong.**

- **No co-founder CEO** (high, fatal). Deadline end-2027, or shelve.
- **Funding not raised, peak need about USD 434k** (high, fatal). Raise in milestone tranches; grants first.
- **Landlords and brokers will not switch** (high, severe). Start with landlords who have empty units; free listings.
- **Brokers push back on scouts** (medium, moderate). LC1 relationships; the advocate's brief.
- **Fake listings or deposit theft** (medium, severe). Verified visits; money held until move-in.
- **Payment rules for collecting rent** (medium, severe). Use a licensed payments partner.
- **Fees too small to cover costs** (medium, severe). Test willingness to pay in the pilot before scaling.
- **Pulls Emlyn from Formcraft** (high, moderate). Cap of 2 hours a week until the CEO starts.

**Stop and fix rules.**

- No CEO by end-2027: shelve, keep the design work.
- Fewer than 300 signed tenancies by end-2028: stop, sell the software, or merge.
- First profitable month more than a year late, with no new funding: wind down in order.

## Soko Foods: big revenue, thin margins

Soko Foods has the most upside and the least room for error. If it survives, the middle run turns over USD 7.4M in 2036, but grain trading keeps only a few cents of each dollar, and one bad season of stock bought high can end it. It is open in 25% of runs in 2036.

**The full strategy.** Soko Foods is now planned as a trading core with four arms: silos (its own and public storage), supply to institutions such as schools and the army, franchised bulk-food shops plus online ordering, and coffee, cocoa and vanilla plantations funded plot by plot by private investors. The shops work more like food banks than supermarkets: families buy the amount they can afford, not a fixed 1 kg pack.

&#91;embedded content: Soko Foods full strategy, October 2026; figures are plan assumptions\]

**Why each arm helps.**

- **Silos** let Soko buy at harvest when prices are low and sell later; public storage and warehouse receipts let farmers borrow against stored grain.
- **Institutions** buy in bulk on contract, so revenue is steadier than open-market trading.
- **Bulk shops and online** reach households directly; franchising grows the network on other people's capital.
- **Plantations** add margin and land value; investor-funded plots spread the cost.

**What each arm adds to the risk.**

- **Institution tenders** (high, severe): slow payment, especially from government; need tender registration and working capital. Cap government supply at a share of revenue.
- **Franchise quality** (medium, severe): one bad shop damages the brand. Written standards, inspections, supply only from Soko.
- **Plantations** (medium, severe): cocoa and vanilla take years to yield, vanilla is a theft target, coffee wilt and cocoa disease are real. Investors must be told in writing when returns start and what can go wrong.
- **Silos** (medium, moderate): storage losses and the cost of building or renting. Start by renting public storage.
- **Too many arms at once** (high, severe): each arm needs its own manager. Open them in order: core, then institutions and shops, then silos and plantations.

* **Best (top 10%): USD 24M or more in 2036.** Group buying spreads across districts; a mill and coffee farms add margin.
* **Base (middle): USD 7.4M.** A solid regional trader with one store and mill.
* **Worst: never launches or closes.** A price crash on full stores, or stock lost to moisture and pests.

**Revenue is not profit.** At the 2036 middle case:

| Net margin | Profit on USD 7.4M |
| --- | --- |
| 2% (plain trading) | About USD 150k |
| 4% (trading plus milling) | About USD 300k |
| 8% (with own coffee and brands) | About USD 590k |

**What could go wrong.**

- **Prices fall after stock is bought** (high, severe). Buy against confirmed orders; use warehouse receipts.
- **Stock spoils: aflatoxin, pests, damp** (high, severe). Moisture meters, hermetic bags, insurance.
- **Drought or a bad season** (medium, severe). Buy from several regions.
- **Farmers sell elsewhere after an advance** (high, moderate). Pay on delivery, no cash advances.
- **Export bans or price controls** (medium, moderate). Serve the home market first.
- **Not enough working capital** (high, severe). Members prepay group orders; a revolving facility.
- **Coffee wilt; 3–4 years before full yield** (medium, severe). Wilt-resistant clonal seedlings; plant in stages.
- **Land title disputes** (medium, severe). Lawyer's search on every title; only clean titles.
- **Disagreement with the CEO partner** (medium, severe). Shareholder agreement and a board seat.

**Stop and fix rules.**

- Pilot saves households less than 5% on staples: redesign before scaling.
- Two loss-making seasons in a row: stop trading; keep the farms.
- No clean-title coffee land by 2029: drop the farm arm.

## Trade Assistant: research, not a business yet

The Trade Assistant has the most honest method of the four (sealed data, random-entry baselines, written specs), but no live evidence yet. The base rates are harsh: only about 7% of people who buy prop challenges are ever paid, and the average payout is about 4% of the account.

**Two separate options.** The tools can earn money two ways, and they can run side by side:

- **Option A, personal trading:** Emlyn trades his own or a prop firm's account. Income depends on a real edge; base rates are harsh (below).
- **Option B, sell the tools to traders:** indicators, expert advisors, the strategy tester and the Replay Trainer, sold on the MQL5 Market or by subscription from 2028. Income depends on customers, not on Emlyn's trading. The retail prop industry is a large market: about [USD 850M in 2026 across 2.1 million funded traders](https://track360.io/blog/prop-trading-industry-statistics-2026), most of them looking for an edge.

In Simulation 2, Option B makes the Trade Assistant a business in 15% of runs by 2036 (against 4% for Option A alone), with middle revenue of about USD 0.6M that year among survivors. Honest selling matters here: no profit promises, published test results, and a clear warning that most buyers of prop challenges lose.

**One challenge, by the numbers.** A USD 100k challenge at FTMO costs about €540:

- **Average buyer (7% paid, 4% payout):** expected payout about USD 280; expected result about USD 300 lost.
- **With a real edge (25% paid):** expected payout about USD 1,000; expected result about USD 400 gained.

So a challenge only pays if the edge is real, which is what the forward test must show first.

- **Best by 2031:** tools sell to outside traders from 2028, and a funded account pays out; both incomes run.
- **Base:** a useful research tool with modest product sales; the forward test shows little edge.
- **Worst:** money and time lost; losses chased with more challenges; hours taken from Formcraft.

**What could go wrong.**

- **Backtests look good, live trading fails** (high, moderate). 100+ demo trades at real size before paying.
- **Chasing losses** (medium, severe). At most two challenge fees a year.
- **Prop firm closes or changes rules** (medium, moderate). Established firms only; withdraw payouts at once.
- **Customers blame the tool for losses** (medium, moderate). Clear terms, no profit claims, published test records.
- **Takes Formcraft time** (high, moderate). Agents maintain; 2 hours a week cap.
- **Platform rules change** (low, moderate). Keep the core platform-neutral.

**Stop and fix rules.**

- After 100 demo trades, if the low end of R per trade is at or below zero: no paid challenge.
- Two failed challenges in a year: pause for six months.
- AI spend over its cap two months running: freeze the agents.

## Pre-mortem: how the billion fails

The most common way to miss the goal is the dullest one: the ventures close early for lack of cash or people, long before growth matters.

&#91;embedded content: Pre-mortem: imagine the failure, then work back to its causes\]

## Personal risks

The person is the one part of the plan that has no spare. Emlyn is 24, an orphan without a family safety net, running five ventures and studying, with every health warning sign from the main tab present. These risks can end all four ventures at once.

- **Health and sleep.** Best: fixed habits by mid-2027 and yearly checkups. Worst: a collapse in a busy year stops every venture.
- **Burnout.** Best: one CEO per venture by 2029. Worst: running everything personally into his 30s.
- **Time.** Best: Formcraft first, caps on the rest. Worst: master's, five ventures and trading all at once.
- **Money.** Best: emergency fund full, company money separate. Worst: a failed venture or trading loss takes his savings.
- **No family safety net.** Best: insurance and three months saved. Worst: one emergency becomes a crisis.
- **Ambition.** Best: the billion as a direction. Worst: chasing the number leads to risky bets.
- **Key relationships.** Best: clear agreements with every partner. Worst: a dispute splits a company.

**The rule this section adds.** If a year ends with two or more health warning signs still present, the next year adds no new venture, course or division.

## Decision rules

Each venture faces the same yearly test, written down now so that hope does not rewrite it later. Cash comes first, then the gate; two misses in a row end it.
