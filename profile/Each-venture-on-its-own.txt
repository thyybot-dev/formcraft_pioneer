Each venture on its own
  Oct 29, 2026            · ​@emlyn

With AI in every venture, a YouTube channel and sales funnel for each, and expansion
from Kampala to the world, Soko Foods alone reaches USD 1 billion of lifetime revenue in
about one run in five, and Formcraft in one in eight. In 2036 every venture is still close to
its Kampala ceiling; the markets beyond are 10 to 100 times larger, so the limit for the next
decade is execution, cash and people, not market size.

How to read each section. Results come from the full-plan simulation (400,000 runs),
read venture by venture. Ceilings are the largest yearly revenue each market could
plausibly support for one firm at each stage, from Kampala to the world, built from the

the business. Every venture also gets a YouTube channel that feeds its AI-automated
website.


Formcraft
Formcraft is the venture most likely to last, and the one whose ceiling is set by software
rather than buildings. In the middle run it earns USD 2.8M in 2036; the best runs, where
Formcraft Tools sell worldwide, pass USD 1 billion of lifetime revenue on their own (12% of
runs).

Results alone.

     Still open in 2036: 55% of runs.
     2036 revenue if open: middle USD 2.8M; best 10% USD 6.4M. By 2041: middle USD 7.1M;
     best 10% USD 26M.
     Lifetime revenue: middle USD 15M; best 10% USD 1.6 billion.
     USD 100M alone: 30%. USD 1 billion alone: 12%.

Ceiling by stage (estimates). Uganda needs about 300,000 new homes a year and spends
14.6% of its budget on transport and infrastructure (US Commercial Guide).

     Kampala, about USD 10M a year: design-build, workshop and concrete for homes,
     rentals and clinics. Rivals: informal draughtsmen, local practices.
     Uganda, about USD 30M: public tenders, oil-region and upcountry projects. Rivals:

What limits it.

 1. Registration. Stamped drawings need registered architects and engineers, who are
    engaged per project until Emlyn or a partner qualifies.
 2. Cash for building. Design-build needs working capital for materials while clients pay
    late.
 3. People. Few staff can work to Formcraft's BIM standard; training takes years.
 4. Emlyn's time, split across five ventures.
 5. Trust for big jobs. Large clients and government want a track record and audited
    accounts.

Touchpoints, in the order a client meets them.

 1. Instagram, X, website and referrals: the first impression; show finished work and
    process.
 2. First call or WhatsApp: answered the same day, with a clear next step.
 3. Site visit and brief: measured, photographed and written up within 48 hours.
 4. Proposal: a fixed-price package, not a vague quote.
 5. Design reviews: renders and walk-throughs the client can understand.
 6. Contract and deposit: simple terms, staged payments.
 7. Workshop and site updates: photos and progress on a client dashboard.
 8. Handover: a building or furniture manual and warranty.
 9. Aftercare: a check-in at 3 and 12 months, and the referral ask.

Who it competes with.

     Informal draughtsmen and fundis: cheapest, no coordination.
     Established architecture practices: reputation and registration.
     Large contractors on big projects, often foreign.

How to win.

     Sell certainty: BIM-coordinated drawings that build without surprises, fixed packages,
     published timelines.

     match.
     Be fast: AI tools turn drawings and quantities around in days, not weeks.
     One roof: design, workshop and build in one contract, one point of responsibility.
     Specialise: healthcare, schools, and rental blocks for landlords (a natural link to
     Nyumba).

YouTube channel and funnel. Formcraft's channel teaches BIM, Revit and AI-in-Revit to a
global audience, and shows projects and furniture from sketch to handover. Education is
the best-paid YouTube niche, with a median of about USD 10 per 1,000 views. Each video
links to a free Revit family or template, then email, then paid Tools, templates and design
inquiries through the AI-automated website.


Soko Foods
Soko Foods with its full plan is the strongest venture on its own. Some part of it is still
running in 58% of runs in 2036, and it reaches USD 1 billion of lifetime revenue alone in
19% of runs, because food is a market that exists in every country it can reach.

Results alone (full plan).

     Still running in 2036: 58% of runs (any arm).
     2036 revenue if running: middle USD 7.1M; best 10% USD 29M. By 2041: middle USD
     12M; best 10% USD 66M.
     Lifetime revenue: middle USD 34M; best 10% USD 3.8 billion.
     USD 100M alone: 41%. USD 1 billion alone: 19%.

Ceiling by stage (estimates). Uganda's smallholders grow about 5.6 million tonnes of
grain a year, worth roughly USD 1.4–2 billion; school meals reach only about 1.2 million of
11.4 million learners.

     Kampala, about USD 30M a year: bulk shops, community buying, institutions in the
     city. Rivals: open markets, supermarkets.
     Uganda, about USD 150M: national grain buying, silos, schools and the army. Rivals:
     large traders and millers.

     East Africa, about USD 600M: cross-border grain to Kenya, South Sudan and Rwanda;
     franchise shops abroad. Rivals: regional grain houses.
     Africa, about USD 2 billion: food security contracts and franchise networks continent-
     wide.
     World, USD 3 billion and up: coffee, cocoa and vanilla exports from Soko plantations,
     competing with global commodity traders on traceability and quality.

What limits it.

 1. Working capital: stock is cash sitting in a store.
 2. Margins of 2–8%: revenue is large, profit is thin.
 3. Storage capacity and quality: damp and aflatoxin destroy value.
 4. Prices and weather: one bad season can wipe out a year.
 5. Tenders: registration rules and slow government payment.
 6. Plantation lag: Robusta takes 3–4 years to reach peak yield.

Touchpoints.

     Farmers: the buying point, an honest scale, payment on delivery by mobile money,
     price messages by SMS.
     Community-buying members: a WhatsApp group, the order, collection day.
     Shoppers: the bulk shop where they scoop and weigh any amount, online orders,
     delivery through the Logistics venture.
     Institutions: the tender, the delivery schedule, quality certificates, the invoice.
     Plantation investors: the plot agreement, quarterly reports, farm visits, payouts.

Who it competes with.

     Farm-gate middlemen, who buy cheaply at harvest.
     Large grain traders and millers.
     Supermarkets and packaged-flour brands.
     Open markets, on price.
     Aid-agency and NGO buyers for institutional supply.

How to win.

     Pay farmers fairly and fast: commercial buyers already pay about 8% more for quality
     maize than local traders; add honest scales and same-day mobile money.
     Sell quality: cleaned, dried, tested grain to East African grades.
     Steady prices for members, using the silos to buy at harvest.
     Own the last mile through the Logistics venture.
     Forecast with AI: prices, demand and stock levels.

     with schools.

YouTube channel and funnel. Farm-to-table stories, grain-quality explainers, food-price
updates, and plantation diaries for coffee, cocoa and vanilla that show investors their
plots growing. Videos link to community-buying sign-up, the online shop, institutional

Logistics
Logistics is easy to start and hard to scale. Some part of it is still running in 42% of runs in
2036, but the middle result then is about USD 0.4M a year; the big outcome needs the
truck app to work in several cities, which happens in only a few runs (3% reach USD 1
billion).

Results alone.

     Still running in 2036: 42% of runs.
     2036 revenue if running: middle USD 0.4M; best 10% USD 2.7M. By 2041: middle USD
     0.6M; best 10% USD 5.8M.
     Lifetime revenue: best 10% USD 98M.
     USD 100M alone: 10%. USD 1 billion alone: 3%.

Ceiling by stage (estimates). Uganda's logistics and warehousing market was about USD
1.24 billion in 2025, heading to USD 2.36 billion by 2032.

     Kampala, about USD 5M a year: e-boda business deliveries and truck booking for
     building sites. Rivals: SafeBoda, boda stages, truck brokers.
     Uganda, about USD 12M: upcountry routes for grain and building materials. Rivals:
     established carriers.
     East Africa, about USD 60M: the Northern Corridor to Kenya, Rwanda and South
     Sudan. Rivals: regional truck marketplaces and 3PLs.
     Africa, about USD 250M: the booking app in other African cities.
     World, about USD 500M: licensing the booking and dispatch software to operators
     elsewhere.

What limits it.

 1. Truck owners' trust: they join only if they are paid fast and fairly.
 2. Cash-flow mismatch: clients pay late, drivers want cash now.
 3. City rules on bodas and the reach of battery-swap networks.
 4. Competition from SafeBoda and informal brokers.
 5. Thin margins per trip.

Touchpoints.

     Business clients: the booking (WhatsApp, then app), the rider's arrival in uniform with
     ID, the live tracking link, photo proof of delivery, the monthly invoice.
     Riders and truck owners: onboarding, the job offer, loading, and payout within 48
     hours.

Who it competes with.

     SafeBoda's business portal for small deliveries.
     Informal boda stages and truck brokers at hardware shops.
     Established logistics firms for larger contracts.
     Big companies' own fleets.

How to win.

     Own a niche: building materials and bulk business deliveries, where SafeBoda is not
     built to serve.
     Guaranteed time windows, with a refund if missed.
     Pay drivers within 48 hours, never before the client pays.
     Plug into the family: Formcraft and Soko orders book trucks automatically.
     Transparent prices shown before booking.
     Lower cost per kilometre with electric bikes.

YouTube channel and funnel. A day on the road with riders and trucks, construction-
logistics tips, and the real economics of electric bodas. Videos link to business sign-up
and the booking app; rider and truck-owner recruitment runs through the same channel.


Nyumba
Nyumba is a strong business where it launches, and most African cities are majority-
renter, so it can travel. Where it is running in 2036 (29% of runs), the middle result is USD
3.5M a year; it reaches USD 100M of lifetime revenue in 15% of runs. These figures include
the USD 5 landlord connection fee.

Results alone.

     Still running in 2036: 29% of runs.
     2036 revenue if running: middle USD 3.5M; best 10% USD 11M. By 2041: middle USD
     6.6M; best 10% USD 30M.
     Lifetime revenue: best 10% USD 318M.
     USD 100M alone: 15%. USD 1 billion alone: 6%.

Ceiling by stage (estimates). About 71% of Kampala households rent; the common single
room rents for about USD 40 a month, and the fastest-moving homes for USD 70–200.

     Kampala, about USD 10M a year: a quarter of about 700,000 renting households at
     about USD 60 a year in fees. Rivals: brokers, Rentify, Facebook groups.
     Uganda, about USD 15M: Entebbe, Mukono, Jinja, Mbarara, Gulu.
     East Africa, about USD 60M: Nairobi, where more than 80% rent, plus Kigali and Dar es
     Salaam. Rivals: established Kenyan listing sites.
     Africa, about USD 300M: Lagos, Accra, Abidjan, where most city households rent.
     World, about USD 400M: management services for African diaspora landlords living
     abroad.

What limits it.

 1. Landlord adoption: without listings there is nothing to rent.
 2. Brokers, who lose income and push back.
 3. Trust and fraud: fake listings and deposit theft.
 4. Payment rules for collecting rent.
 5. Low rents, which keep each fee small.

Touchpoints.

     Tenants: the search, verified photos, booking a viewing, the unlock fee, move-in with
     the Rent Passport and condition record, rent reminders, repairs, move-out and deposit
     return.
     Landlords: onboarding with the USD 5 connection fee, the listing, tenant screening,
     rent collection, and monthly reports for landlords abroad.

Who it competes with.

     Brokers and commission agents.
     Rental apps such as Rentify.
     Facebook groups and classifieds.
     Property managers for larger blocks.

How to win.

     No broker fee for tenants, and listings verified in person.
     The Rent Passport: a good-tenant record that follows the tenant.
     A fee landlords barely notice: USD 5 per connection.
     Fast repairs through vetted fundis.
     A dashboard for landlords abroad.

Trade Assistant
Sold as tools to traders worldwide, the Trade Assistant is a small, global business. It is
running in 19% of runs in 2036, earning a middle USD 0.8M that year; its real value is skill,
credibility, side income and the content it gives the YouTube channel.

Results alone (tools sold from 2028).

     A business in 2036: 19% of runs.
     2036 revenue if running: middle USD 0.8M; best 10% USD 2.6M. By 2041: middle USD
     1.3M; best 10% USD 6M.
     USD 100M alone: 5%. USD 1 billion alone: 1.6%.

Ceiling: about USD 10M a year, sold worldwide from day one (estimate). On
MetaTrader's marketplace, the best sellers earn over USD 10,000 a month among more
than 10,000 registered sellers, and the retail prop-trading industry is about USD 850M a
year. The market is global, so there are no local stages: it competes with every seller
worldwide from the start.

What limits it.

 1. Trust: the market is full of exaggerated claims.
 2. Crowding: thousands of sellers.
 3. Platform dependence on MetaQuotes and prop-firm rules.
 4. Edge decay: strategies stop working.
 5. Regulation of trading products in some countries.

Touchpoints.

     The product page with honest test results.
     A free demo or trial.
     Purchase and setup.
     Updates, support and a user community.
     Refunds, handled fairly.

Who it competes with.

YouTube channels
The five channels are worth far more as a sales engine than as income. Their own revenue
reaches a middle USD 0.2M a year by 2036 (best 10%: USD 0.9M), but the visibility they
bring adds 11 points to the lifetime billion odds, against 1 point from the ad money.

Results.

     Still active in 2036: 36% of runs (most channels stop within a few years; consistency is
     the whole game).
     Yearly income if active: 2031 middle USD 61k; 2036 middle USD 213k; 2041 middle USD
     459k, best 10% USD 2.6M.
     Lifetime income: best 10% USD 35M.

What the record says. The top 10% of channels take 58% of all ad revenue, only about
4.3% of creators make more than USD 100,000 a year, and Education pays a median of
about USD 10 per 1,000 views against about USD 2 for business and finance. Content
marketing produces leads at about 62% lower cost than outbound selling.

Ceiling by stage (estimates). Kampala and Uganda audiences earn little per view: about
USD 0.2–0.5M a year for all channels. East Africa and Africa, about USD 1–3M. A global
audience, mainly for Formcraft's BIM education and the trading tools, could reach USD
10M a year with sponsorships and courses.

What limits it. Consistency (most channels stop), low ad rates for African audiences,
Emlyn's time on camera, and finance-content rules for the trading channel.
